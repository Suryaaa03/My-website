Predicting HDB Resale Prices with Machine Learning

Comparing linear regression, decision trees, k-NN and principal component regression to predict HDB resale prices.

My role
Individual project
Team
Individual (DSE1101, NUS)
When
Nov 2024
Report & files ↓

Overview

Buyers and sellers make better decisions when they understand what drives HDB resale prices. Working with a dataset of flat characteristics, I reduced 230 candidate predictors to 133, then built and compared four predictive models, judging them by RMSE and margin of error against the mean price of about $497,000.

What I did

  1. Log-transformed the right-skewed resale price to stabilise variance and reduce the pull of extreme values.
  2. Used a 4,000/2,000 train-test split and 10-fold cross-validation for every model.
  3. Built a linear model with forward selection (91 predictors), a decision tree tuned on the complexity parameter, and k-NN tuned on k.
  4. Applied PCA to keep 95% of the variance, cutting 133 predictors to 69 components, and fitted a principal component regression.

Results

  • Principal component regression was the most accurate: RMSE $40,092, an 8.06% margin of error
  • Beat linear regression (10.34%), decision tree (11.07%) and k-NN (15.12%)
  • Discussed limitations and next steps, such as ridge regression and adding economic factors like housing grants
