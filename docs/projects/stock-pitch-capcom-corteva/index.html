Nanyang Capital Stock Pitch: Capcom vs Corteva

We built full buy cases for two very different stocks, Capcom and Corteva, then chose to pitch Corteva (BUY, US$66.33 target).

Overview

A stock pitch only gets one pick, so we analysed two candidates in full before committing: Capcom (TSE: 9697), the Japanese games publisher behind Resident Evil and Monster Hunter, and Corteva (NYSE: CTVA), the US seed and crop protection company spun out of DowDuPont.

Each got the full treatment: a revenue model built from business segments, a discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation, a peer comparison and a risk review. With both cases side by side, we decided to pitch Corteva.

What I did

  1. Capcom: compiled five years of financials and projected revenue by segment (Digital Contents, Arcade Operations, Amusement Equipment, Other) to FY2028, driven by unit sales of major titles and catalog growth.
  2. Capcom: valued the company with a DCF (WACC 8.7%, terminal growth 2.5%) weighted with peer multiples and probability-weighted bear, base and bull scenarios, and wrote a variant view on five market concerns.
  3. Corteva: split revenue into seed and crop protection, forecast each by region from price, volume and currency effects, and modelled restructuring savings and their effect on margins.
  4. Corteva: built a DCF (CAPM cost of equity, 2.5% terminal growth) with a WACC and terminal-growth sensitivity table, then cross-checked it against a football field of peer multiples.
  5. Assessed each company's competitive position (moat, Porter's five forces) and key risks, then compared the two cases on upside, downside and conviction.

Results & conclusions

  • Final pick: Corteva, BUY with a US$66.33 target (+25.7%)
  • Corteva's downside valuation (US$56.10) still sat above its share price
  • Capcom case: BUY with a ¥4,800 base target (+30.8%), but a bear case of −24%

Conclusion

Both companies came out as BUYs. Capcom offered more upside in the base case (+30.8%) but a wider spread of outcomes: its bear case implied a 24% loss if game launches disappointed. Corteva's base case offered +25.7%, its target sat slightly above the median of the peer-multiple ranges, and even its downside valuation of US$56.10 was above the share price at the time. After weighing both, we pitched Corteva.

What I learnt

Running two full analyses side by side taught me that a stock pitch is as much about the downside as the upside. I learnt to build revenue from business-segment drivers instead of extrapolating totals, to cross-check a DCF against market multiples (a football field makes it obvious when a target is out of line), and how sensitive a DCF is to WACC and terminal growth. Writing the variant view for Capcom forced me to state exactly where we disagreed with the market and what would prove us right.
