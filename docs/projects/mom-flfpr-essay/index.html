Raising Singapore's Female Labour Force Participation

NTU x MOM essay competition: a data-driven country similarity index and a policy proposal for Singapore.

My role
Built the machine learning similarity index; co-wrote the policy analysis
Team
Co-authored with Clarence Chow
When
Jun – Jul 2025
Report & files ↓

Overview

Singapore's female labour force participation rate (61.25% in 2024) lags global leaders like Iceland (70.3%). Policies only transfer well between similar countries, so before studying what other countries do, we used data to find the countries most like Singapore.

We then evaluated their parental leave, childcare, equal pay and four-day-week policies through three economic frameworks, and designed a proposal suited to Singapore.

What I did

  1. Combined country-level data on female participation trends, female working hours, GDP per capita, purchasing power, cost of living and a broader similarity index.
  2. Cleaned and matched country names across sources with fuzzy matching, and imputed missing values from the nearest similar countries.
  3. Weighted each metric by its correlation with female participation and scored every country's distance from Singapore, identifying Iceland, New Zealand and Vietnam as the closest comparators.
  4. Evaluated their policies using Human Capital Theory, Feminist Economics and Institutional Path Dependence.
  5. Proposed Community-Supported Workplace Childcare Co-operatives: employer-supported, government-subsidised childcare hubs with AI-driven scheduling and a 32-hour work week.

Results

  • Data-driven selection of comparator countries rather than picking them by intuition
  • A concrete, Singapore-specific policy proposal grounded in evidence from Iceland, New Zealand, Vietnam and Quebec
